How to track fiber in MyFitnessPal

MyFitnessPal knows the fiber content of nearly everything you log. It just doesn't consider it interesting enough to show you. Here's exactly where the number lives, how to get it closer to the surface, what costs money, and the two data problems that make a lot of people's fiber totals quietly wrong.

Quick Answer

To see fiber in MyFitnessPal: open your Diary, scroll to the bottom and tap Nutrition, then choose the Nutrients tab - fiber is listed there with your total for the day. This works on the free tier. Putting fiber on your main dashboard in place of a default macro, and setting a custom fiber goal, are Premium features on current versions. Watch out for user-submitted foods with fiber left at 0g, which silently undercount you.

Where fiber actually lives

  1. Open the Diary tab and log your food as normal.
  2. Scroll to the bottom of the diary and tap Nutrition.
  3. Switch to the Nutrients tab (the default view is usually Calories or Macros).
  4. Fiber appears in the list with your running total for the day, and on most versions with a percentage against a default goal.

That's it, and it's free. The catch is that it's four interactions away from where you already are, which in practice means you check it in week one and forget by week three. Visibility is most of what makes tracking change behavior.

Set a fiber goal (Premium)

If you have Premium: Goals, then Nutrition Goals, then Additional Nutrients, and set a fiber target. Use your real number rather than the default - the calculator is here, but as a rule it's 14g per 1,000 calories you eat.

On free, you're stuck with the default goal, which is generally around the 25-28g mark. Fine as a rough line, wrong if you eat significantly more or less than 2,000 calories.

The two data problems that make totals wrong

1. Crowdsourced entries with 0g fiber

MyFitnessPal's database is enormous largely because users add foods, and users adding foods frequently fill in calories, protein, carbs and fat, then leave fiber blank. That entry then reads as 0g fiber forever.

Log a cup of black beans from a bad entry and you've just lost 15g off your daily total without any indication anything went wrong. Fix: prefer entries with a verification checkmark or one that clearly came from the manufacturer, and spot-check anything you eat often. If your favourite bread entry says 0g, it's wrong - find another.

2. Recipes and restaurant meals

Multi-ingredient entries are the worst offenders, because whoever built them may have estimated. A restaurant burrito bowl entry showing 3g of fiber when it contains a cup of beans is not unusual. For anything mixed, either log the components separately or treat the fiber number as a guess.

A two-minute accuracy audit

1. Open a typical logged day and go to Nutrition > Nutrients.

2. Scan your entries for anything containing beans, whole grains, fruit with skin, or vegetables.

3. Any of those showing 0g fiber is a bad entry. Replace it and save the corrected one to your favourites.

4. Re-check your total. Most people find they were undercounting by 4-8g a day.

Workarounds if you're staying on free

  • Build a favourites list of verified high-fiber foods so you're re-logging known-good entries rather than searching fresh each time.
  • Check Nutrients once at dinner, not at every meal. One look tells you whether the evening needs a fiber-heavy side.
  • Learn five anchor numbers - a cup of black beans is about 15g, half a cup of dry oats about 4g, a pear with skin about 5.5g, a cup of raspberries about 8g, a cup of cooked broccoli about 5g. With those memorised you can estimate without opening anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does MyFitnessPal track fiber?

Yes. Log your food as usual, then open the Diary, scroll to the bottom, tap Nutrition, and switch to the Nutrients tab - fiber is listed there with your daily total. This works on the free tier. Putting fiber on your main dashboard or setting a custom fiber goal are Premium features on current versions.

How do I add fiber to my MyFitnessPal dashboard?

That requires Premium on current versions. Go to Goals, Nutrition Goals, and Additional Nutrients, where you can set a fiber target and swap fiber into one of the main dashboard slots in place of a default macro. On the free tier you can still see fiber, but only by tapping into the Nutrients view rather than at a glance.

Why does MyFitnessPal show 0g fiber for some foods?

Because a large part of the database is user-submitted, and many people entering a food fill in calories and macros while leaving fiber blank - which stores as zero rather than as unknown. This silently undercounts you, sometimes by a lot if the food is beans or whole grains. Prefer verified entries, and spot-check anything you eat regularly.

Is there a better app than MyFitnessPal for fiber?

For fiber specifically, yes - a dedicated tracker puts the number on the front screen instead of three taps deep, and typically uses USDA-based values rather than crowdsourced ones. FiberUp is free and also tracks weekly plant variety, which no calorie app does. If you need calories and fiber in one place, Cronometer's free tier is the better all-rounder.
