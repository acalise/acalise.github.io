All-Bran vs Fiber One: which cereal actually wins?

These are the two cereals people buy when they've decided to do something about their fiber intake, and the comparison usually gets settled by whichever number on the box is bigger. That's a mistake. Fiber One Original does have more grams per serving than most things in the aisle - but it gets a large share of them from added chicory root inulin, which behaves completely differently in your gut than the wheat bran in All-Bran. Same number on the label, different experience by mid-morning.

Quick Answer

Fiber One Original Bran has the highest fiber per serving of the two - roughly 18g in a half-cup, against roughly 12g for All-Bran Original and about 13g in a smaller serving of All-Bran Buds. But Fiber One gets much of that from added chicory root inulin, which is heavily fermented and is the single most common cause of "I ate a high-fiber cereal and now I'm bloated." All-Bran Buds is the better choice for constipation, because it combines wheat bran with psyllium. If you bloat easily, start with All-Bran and a half portion. Always check the box - manufacturers reformulate, and figures differ between the US, UK, and other regions.

Second, All-Bran Buds delivers close to Fiber One numbers in a noticeably smaller serving, which matters if you find bran cereal hard to get through.

Why the fiber source decides more than the number

All fiber is not one substance. The relevant distinction here is how much of it your gut bacteria eat.

  • Wheat bran is mostly insoluble and poorly fermented. It passes through largely intact, adding bulk and speeding transit. Low gas.
  • Psyllium is soluble and gel-forming but only partially fermented. It softens and bulks at the same time. Low to moderate gas.
  • Chicory root inulin is soluble and completely fermented. Your bacteria digest all of it, and gas is the byproduct. It's a genuine prebiotic with real benefits - and it's also why a bowl of certain high-fiber cereals will have you uncomfortable by 10am.

This is the whole comparison in one paragraph. Fiber One Original's headline number is partly built on inulin. If you tolerate inulin well, it's an efficient way to hit a target. If you don't, the higher number is actively working against you, and All-Bran's boring wheat bran is the better product despite losing on paper.

How to tell which camp you're in

Eat a half portion of Fiber One Original for three days. If you're fine, you tolerate inulin and can use the higher fiber density. If you're gassy and bloated by day two, that's the inulin, not "fiber" in general - switch to a bran-and-psyllium cereal and you'll likely be fine at the same gram count.

For constipation specifically: All-Bran Buds

If constipation is the reason you're standing in the cereal aisle, All-Bran Buds is the more targeted answer, and it's because of the psyllium. Wheat bran alone adds bulk and speeds things along, which helps. Psyllium adds bulk and holds water as a gel, which softens - and hard stool is usually the actual problem rather than insufficient volume.

It's essentially the mechanism of a psyllium supplement, delivered in a bowl. See the best fiber for constipation for the fuller picture, and Metamucil vs Benefiber if you'd rather take it as a powder.

Sugar, sodium, and the rest of the label

Fiber content is one number on a panel with several that matter. A few things worth checking on whichever box you pick up:

  • Sugar varies enormously across both lines. The plain bran versions of both are low; the flavoured, clustered, and frosted variants are a different product nutritionally. If you're comparing, compare the specific box, not the brand.
  • Sodium is higher than people expect in bran cereals generally.
  • What you add matters more than the difference between these two. A tablespoon of berries and some milk changes the meal more than three grams of cereal fiber does.

The thing neither box tells you

Both of these cereals will get you to a daily fiber target faster than almost anything else you can eat. That's genuinely useful, and it's also a narrow win. A single cereal is one or two fiber types; the benefits associated with high-fiber diets in the research come from varied plant intake - beans, oats, fruit, vegetables, nuts - which delivers dozens of fiber types plus everything else attached to them.

The sensible use of a bran cereal is as a floor: it guarantees you start the day at 12-18g so the rest of the day only has to add, not rescue. It's a poor strategy as the entire plan. More on that in high-fiber breakfast ideas and the full rundown in best high-fiber cereals.

The verdict

Pick by your situation

You want maximum fiber per bowl and your gut is robust - Fiber One Original Bran.

You're constipated - All-Bran Buds, for the psyllium.

You bloat easily or you're new to high-fiber eating - All-Bran Original, half portion, build up over two weeks.

You want it to taste like breakfast - be honest that the clustered and flaked versions of both lines trade a lot of fiber for that, and check the actual box.

And whichever you buy: increase gradually and drink more water. Jumping from 12g of fiber a day to 30g overnight is uncomfortable regardless of which cereal caused it. See how to increase fiber without gas.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which has more fiber, All-Bran or Fiber One?

Fiber One Original Bran has more per serving - roughly 18g in a half cup, compared with roughly 10-12g for All-Bran Original and about 13g for a smaller serving of All-Bran Buds. Be careful which Fiber One you are comparing, though: the flakes and clusters varieties have around half the fiber of Fiber One Original Bran. Figures also differ between regions and change when manufacturers reformulate, so check the box you are holding.

Why does Fiber One make me bloated?

Most likely the chicory root inulin. Fiber One gets a large share of its fiber from added inulin, which is completely fermented by gut bacteria - and gas is the byproduct of that fermentation. Wheat bran, the main fiber in All-Bran, is poorly fermented and passes through largely intact, which is why it produces much less gas at the same gram count. If a high-fiber cereal bloats you, try a bran-based one before concluding that fiber generally does not agree with you.

Which cereal is better for constipation?

All-Bran Buds, because it combines wheat bran with psyllium. Wheat bran adds bulk and speeds transit; psyllium forms a gel that holds water and softens stool, which addresses the more common underlying problem. That is essentially the mechanism of a psyllium supplement delivered as breakfast. Drink plenty of water with it - a high-fiber cereal without enough fluid can make constipation worse.

Is a high-fiber cereal enough to hit my daily fiber target?

It gets you a long way, which is the point of it. A bowl of Fiber One Original Bran covers roughly half of a typical 25-38g daily target before you have eaten anything else. But relying on one cereal means eating one or two fiber types repeatedly, and the health benefits associated with high-fiber diets come from varied plant intake. Treat the cereal as a reliable floor and build variety across the rest of the day.
