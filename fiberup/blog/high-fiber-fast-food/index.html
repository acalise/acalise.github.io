High-fiber fast food: the best orders at 10 chains

Fast food isn't where fiber goes to thrive - a burger, fries and a soda is close to a rounding error. But the gap between the worst and best order at the same chain is often 15 grams or more, which is over half a day's target decided at the counter. Here's what to order, and the three rules that work anywhere.

Quick Answer

The single best high-fiber fast food strategy is to order something built on beans. A Chipotle burrito bowl with black beans, brown rice, fajita vegetables and guacamole lands somewhere around 15-20g of fiber; a Taco Bell bean burrito or Power Menu Bowl with beans is roughly 9-12g. Beyond beans, look for whole-grain bread, add every vegetable offered, keep skins on potatoes, and treat avocado as a fiber ingredient rather than a fat one.

Three rules that work at any counter

  1. Beans beat everything. No other fast-food ingredient is close. A single scoop of black or pinto beans typically adds 7-8g; a full portion can be 12-15g. If beans are on the menu, they decide your order.
  2. Say yes to every vegetable. Lettuce is nearly nothing, but peppers, onions, corn, tomatoes, spinach and slaw stack up. Free toppings are free fiber and most people decline them.
  3. Whole grain where offered, skins where possible. Whole-grain bread over white is typically +2-3g. A baked potato with the skin is +2g over one without.

Why Chipotle-style bowls win so decisively

It's not the brand, it's the format. A build-your-own bowl lets you stack four separate fiber sources in one order - a legume, a whole grain, roasted vegetables, and avocado - none of which the kitchen has to make a special exception for. Any chain with that format performs well, which is why fast-casual bowl restaurants generally beat traditional fast food by a wide margin.

The build order, in priority sequence

1. Beans. Black or pinto. Non-negotiable, and ask for extra if it's offered.

2. A whole grain base. Brown rice, quinoa, farro. Adds 2-4g over white rice.

3. Every vegetable on offer. Fajita veg, corn salsa, pico, lettuce, greens.

4. Avocado or guacamole. A serving is 4-5g of fiber, which nobody thinks of it as.

5. Skip the flour tortilla. A large white tortilla contributes little fiber and a lot of everything else - bowl format is the better trade.

The traps

  • "Wheat" bread. Not the same as whole wheat. If the first ingredient is enriched flour, you're getting white bread with caramel colour. See what to actually look for in bread.
  • Salads as a fiber strategy. A big bowl of iceberg is around 1g. Lettuce is water. Fiber in a salad comes from beans, seeds, avocado, and dense vegetables - not the leaves.
  • Smoothies with juice bases. Juice has essentially no fiber. A whole-fruit smoothie is fine; a juice-based one is sugar.
  • Fries. A medium portion has a couple of grams, but the ratio of everything-else to fiber is terrible. Not a strategy, just not zero.

If you eat out most days

Two structural fixes matter more than optimising individual orders:

Make one meal a day bean-based. If lunch out reliably includes a scoop of beans, you've secured 10-15g before you get home, and dinner only needs to add a vegetable to get you to target.

Fix breakfast, since it's the meal you control. Oats, berries and seeds is 12-15g before you've left the house, which makes an unavoidable low-fiber lunch survivable. See high-fiber breakfast ideas.

Track it for a week or two and the pattern becomes obvious fast - most people find one or two habitual orders are responsible for nearly all their shortfall.

Frequently Asked Questions

What fast food has the most fiber?

Bean-based orders at build-your-own bowl chains. A Chipotle-style burrito bowl with black beans, brown rice, fajita vegetables and guacamole typically lands around 15-20g, which is over half a day's target in one meal. Taco Bell's bean-based items are the next best value, and Wendy's chili with a skin-on baked potato is a strong option at a traditional fast-food chain.

Is there any fiber in a fast food salad?

Less than people assume. Lettuce and other leaves are mostly water and contribute around a gram, so a large salad built only on greens is close to nothing. The fiber in a salad comes from what you add - beans, chickpeas, avocado, seeds, corn, and dense vegetables. A salad with those can reach 8-10g; one without them rarely clears 3g.

How can I get more fiber when eating out?

Order beans whenever they appear on a menu, accept every vegetable topping offered, choose whole-grain bread or a whole-grain base over white, keep potato skins on, and treat avocado as a fiber ingredient. If you eat out most days, the higher-leverage move is making breakfast a high-fiber meal at home, since it's the one you fully control and it can cover half your daily target on its own.

Does fast food count toward my daily fiber goal?

Yes - fiber is fiber regardless of where it came from, and a bean-heavy fast-food bowl genuinely contributes. What differs is everything travelling alongside it: sodium, saturated fat, and calories are typically much higher than the home-cooked equivalent. The practical approach is to optimise the fast-food meals you're already eating rather than treating them as a write-off.
