How to block Instagram on iPhone without deleting it

Instagram is the awkward one. Unlike TikTok, you probably have real reasons to keep it - messages from people who only use DMs, a group chat, a business account, event details that live nowhere else. So "just delete it" isn't advice, it's a shrug. What you want is surgical: keep the parts you need, cut the two-hour Reels hole. Here's how.

To cut the endless part specifically, turn off Reels suggestions in Instagram's own settings, mute Stories, and use Following mode instead of the algorithmic feed. First: separate the app from the hole

Instagram is at least four products in one trench coat, and only some of them are the problem:

  • DMs - genuinely useful, often the reason you can't delete it.
  • Following feed - finite. You reach the end. It ends.
  • Stories - semi-finite, mildly compulsive.
  • Reels and Explore - infinite, algorithmic, and where roughly all of the lost time lives.

If you block the whole app you also block the useful bit, which is why so many people give up after three days. Aim at Reels and Explore first.

Method 1: kill the infinite parts inside Instagram (free, do this first)

  1. Switch the feed to Following. Tap the Instagram logo at the top left and choose Following. This gives you a feed with an actual end, which is the single most useful change available.
  2. Turn off suggested content. In Settings and privacy, look for Content preferences / Suggested content and reduce or snooze suggestions where the option is offered.
  3. Mute the Stories that pull you in. Long-press an avatar, Mute, Mute Stories. You keep the person, you lose the daily tug.
  4. Turn off all notifications except DMs. Settings, Notifications - kill likes, comments, suggestions, "so-and-so posted." Keep messages if you truly need them.
  5. Use the in-app daily limit. Instagram's own Time Management tools include a reminder and a daily limit. Weak, but free and one layer deeper than nothing.

Done properly this alone cuts most people's Instagram time substantially, because it removes the infinite surface rather than fighting your willpower.

Method 2: Screen Time App Limit (free)

  1. Settings, Screen Time, App Limits, Add Limit.
  2. Expand Social, tick Instagram only.
  3. Set a daily amount you'll actually respect.
  4. Turn on Block at End of Limit and set a Screen Time passcode different from your unlock code.

Method 3: Downtime and Focus (free)

Downtime blacks out a scheduled window. Focus modes go further by hiding the icon entirely - and since a large share of opens are triggered by seeing the icon rather than wanting the app, hiding it is unusually effective for the effort involved.

A good pairing: a Work focus that hides social entirely, and a Wind Down focus from 9pm that does the same and silences notifications.

Method 4: use it on the web, deliberately

Underrated trick. Delete the app, use instagram.com in Safari when you need it. The mobile web version is deliberately worse: clunkier feed, worse Reels experience, more friction on every action. You keep access to messages and posts, and you lose the frictionless infinite scroll. For a lot of people this is the whole fix.

What about just deleting it?

It works, and if you can afford to lose the DMs it's the cleanest answer. Your account, photos and followers all survive - deleting the app is not deleting the account. Most people who "quit Instagram" successfully did exactly this and used the web occasionally. If you can't lose the DMs, the surgical route above gets you most of the benefit. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I block Instagram on iPhone but keep DMs?

There's no clean way to block only the feed at the operating-system level, so do it inside the app instead: switch to the Following feed, mute Stories, turn off suggested content, and disable every notification except messages. That removes the infinite surface while leaving DMs intact. Then add a Screen Time limit or a toll-based blocker on top so even the DM visits stay short.

How do I turn off Reels on Instagram?

You can't remove the Reels tab entirely, but you can starve it: set your feed to Following instead of For You by tapping the logo at the top left, reduce or snooze suggested content in Content preferences, and mark Reels you're shown as Not Interested so the algorithm has less to work with. Combined with a Screen Time limit on the app, this handles most of the lost time.

Is it better to delete Instagram or block it?

Delete it if you can afford to lose direct messages - it's cleaner and your account, photos, and followers all survive since deleting the app isn't deleting the account. Block it if DMs are load-bearing for you. A good middle path is deleting the app and using instagram.com in Safari, which keeps access while making the experience deliberately worse.

Why do I keep reinstalling Instagram after deleting it?

Because deletion removes the app but not the underlying restlessness, and reinstalling costs about ninety seconds. Removal without replacement leaves a hole that the urge will fill with something. The setups that hold long-term either make re-entry genuinely expensive, or give the restless energy somewhere else to go - which is the logic behind exercise-to-unlock blockers.
